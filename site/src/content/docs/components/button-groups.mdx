There are two kinds of button group.

## Connected: pick one (or several)

<Demo kind="connected" caption="Tonal rounded squares; the selected option becomes a primary pill and widens" />

Connected groups look like a segmented control. Build one from `ToggleButton`s with the
connected shapes for the first, middle and last items:

<Snippet region="connected-button-group" />

In Google's examples the selected segment turns dark, becomes a full pill and gets a little wider
while the others shrink. The connected shapes give you the shape change. The width change comes
from `weight` and the group's layout.

## Standard: a row of actions

<Demo kind="group" caption="Press and hold a button: it grows and its neighbours give way" />

`ButtonGroup` lays out actions where the pressed one grows and pushes its neighbours. Items
that don't fit move into an overflow menu automatically.

<Snippet region="button-group-overflow" />

Inside `ButtonGroup`, add items with `clickableItem`, `toggleableItem` or `customItem`.
`ButtonGroupScope` is not a `RowScope`, so plain composables don't go there.

## Accessibility

- Single-select: put `Role.RadioButton` on each item (as in the snippet).
- Multi-select: use `Role.Checkbox`.
- Label every icon-only item.

## Common mistakes

- Using the same `ButtonGroupDefaults.connectedMiddleButtonShapes()` for every item. The first and last items need their own shapes.
- Expecting `SingleChoiceSegmentedButtonRow` to look expressive. It still works, but the connected group is the M3 Expressive look.
